# Fix resolution first, then stack channels early

Researchers who fuse renderings from one spinning or mobile LiDAR almost always do it the simplest way: they stack range, intensity/reflectivity, sometimes xyz, normals and a validity mask, as channels into one network. The controlled studies that exist put the gain from adding intensity to geometry at **about +0.4 to +2.6 mIoU in-domain**. The gains are larger for small or rare classes, and pole classes go either way. For PoleSight, with 870 training images, one dominant modality and pixel-perfect registration, the evidence favours three moves in order. **First, fix effective resolution**, because downscaling 1024×128 frames to 640×80 is the largest measured lever for objects this small. **Second, stack intensity, range and a validity mask into a 3-channel early-fusion input** that keeps the COCO-pretrained stem. **Third, benchmark that against non-learned late fusion of two single-modality models.** Multi-stage, two-stream mid fusion, which the earlier `fusion_sota.md` draft named the "main candidate", is not supported as the first bet. On the only small-data fusion-stage ablation (about 1.1k images, YOLOv5s), multi-stage feature fusion scored **10 mAP50 below pixel-level fusion**. The only same-backbone range-view test found that splitting intensity and geometry into separate stems gained **0.0 and +1.0 mIoU** in-domain. A CMX control showed that a second stream fed pure noise adds +1.0 mIoU, so small mid-fusion gains cannot be credited to the second modality without controls. Training choices are measured to matter as much as or more than the fusion operator: range-view mixing augmentation (+6 to +10 mIoU), the 16-bit-to-8-bit encoding (up to 12.8 mAP spread on thermal) and avoiding gradient-balancing methods when one modality dominates. No published work fuses intensity and range for *instance segmentation of thin poles* in MLS range images, so nearly every PoleSight-specific effect size is still unknown, and PoleSight experiments would be the first evidence.

## Evidence labels used in this report

Each recommendation carries one of three statuses. **Established** means a primary source measured it in a comparable setting. **Inferred** means it follows from measured results in a different setting (another task, sensor or modality pair). **Unknown** means no source measured it and PoleSight would produce the first evidence. Strength is rated **Strong** (two or more controlled studies read in full text, consistent direction), **Moderate** (one controlled full-text study, or several consistent but secondary or indirect ones), or **Weak** (abstract- or snippet-level, a distant analogue, or a fetch-tool summary the research team could not check against the raw table). Some numbers below came through a summarising fetch tool rather than from the paper's table. They are flagged "(secondary)" and should be rechecked before they are quoted in a paper. PoleSight's own figures come from the repository (`results/result.md`, `data/dataset_analysis_report.json`). The Ultralytics code facts (long-side resize to `imgsz`, `intersect_dicts` dropping the first conv when `channels != 3`, HSV augmentation remixing packed channels, fixed `/255` normalisation) were verified against the installed `ultralytics==8.4.19` source in earlier work and are reused as verified.

## Same-sensor LiDAR channels are almost always stacked, and the gain is 0.4 to 2.6 points

The range-view segmentation literature treats channel stacking as the default. RangeNet++, SalsaNext, CENet, FIDNet, MaskRange and RangeViT all feed a 5-channel image (x, y, z, range, remission), sometimes with a mask or normals added, into one encoder. Most of them never ablate the channels. The ablations that do exist agree on direction. **Intensity is a modest in-domain gain.** CENet drops from 61.4 to 58.8 mIoU on SemanticKITTI without reflectivity ([Sanchez et al., ICCV 2023, Table 3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.04245)). SalsaNext loses 1.5 and RangeViT 0.4 mIoU when restricted to geometry ([Yang et al. 2025, Tables 4–5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.08979)). On older three-class KITTI tasks the dominant class loses more: SqueezeSeg car IoU falls from 64.6 to 57.1 without intensity ([SqueezeSeg](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.07368)), and SalsaNet vehicle IoU from 71.44 to 66.06 ([SalsaNet, Table III](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.08291)). Waymo vehicle IoU is 84.8 with intensity and 83.4 without ([Guillard et al. 2022, Table III](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.10986)). Status: **established, Strong**, for semantic segmentation of driving scans.

PoleSight's own gap sits in the same band. Range-only beats intensity-only on 14 of 15 YOLO-seg checkpoints by a mean of **+1.28 mask mAP50-95** and on all 15 by **+3.60 box mAP50-95**. Intensity wins only on `fence_pole` (8.91 vs 7.09 mask mAP50-95, intensity ahead on 13 of 15 checkpoints) (`results/result.md`). The literature's intensity contribution and PoleSight's inter-modality gap are of similar size. That is an argument for expecting **a fusion gain on the order of one or two mask points, not a step change**. It is an inference (Moderate), and seed variance of the same order makes it hard to detect (see the plan section).

Three other channels have clearer evidence than intensity. **The validity mask is the cheapest high-value channel.** Removing it from SalsaNet's front-view input cut vehicle IoU from 71.44 to 59.53 and average IoU from 79.71 to 75.20 ([SalsaNet, Table III](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.08291)). Adding it to SqueezeSegV2 raised cyclist IoU from 25.4 to 32.3 and average IoU from 37.4 to 39.8 ([SqueezeSegV2, Table I](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.08495)). Status: **established, Moderate** (two older papers, consistent; the gain concentrates on small or ragged objects). **Filling missing pixels** with range-aware nearest-neighbour interpolation raised four range-view networks by +2.2 to +3.3 mIoU on SemanticKITTI ([FMVNet, Table 11](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.10175), secondary). **Explicit xyz adds little** once range and pixel position are known. SalsaNet scored slightly *better* without the Y channel (80.05 vs 79.71 average). Triess et al. dropped xyz because it did not "influence the performance in a significant way". Meta-RangeSeg argues xyz is implicit in the image coordinates ([Meta-RangeSeg](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.13377)). PoleSight releases no xyz, so this is reassuring rather than limiting (inferred, Moderate). Normals computed from range-image gradients added +1.71 mIoU at 128×2048 on an Ouster OS2-128 ([Reichert et al. 2025, Table V](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.21602), secondary), which is the only measurement at PoleSight's 128-row vertical resolution (Weak).

The "LiDAR as a camera" detection literature also stacks channels, into the three RGB slots of COCO-pretrained YOLOs. The closest analogue to PoleSight is **Casado-Coscolla et al. (J. Imaging 2024)**. It ran YOLOv8-seg n–x on Ouster 1024×128 images, ablated all seven subsets of ambient, range and reflectivity, and concluded that "the combination of both [range and reflectivity] is, on average, the one that provides the highest scores". Reflectivity led at loose IoU and range matched or beat it at mAP50:95 ([PMC11728245](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11728245/)). The per-subset values exist only in Fig. 5 and were not extracted. The target was large indoor persons, not thin poles. Status: **established direction, Moderate strength** for "stacked range + reflectivity ≥ best single channel in YOLO-seg on same-sensor LiDAR images". The NTNU SnowPole extended evaluation (six YOLO models, Ouster OS2-128 snow poles) reports that pseudo-colour combinations beat single channels "across most configurations". That is abstract-level only and did not include range ([Mendeley](https://data.mendeley.com/datasets/tt6rbx7s3h/3); Weak). No paper found reports a same-sensor channel combination clearly *worse* than its best constituent in 2D detection. Some weaker signals do point that way. Ambient is "noisy" and contributes least ([Casado-Coscolla](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11728245/)). For ALS tree detection, a single density raster reportedly beat a density + height + intensity stack ([Windrim & Bryson 2020](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/12/9/1469), secondary).

On **thin and pole classes specifically**, the evidence is mixed and small. Replacing raw intensity with calibrated reflectivity moved SemanticKITTI pole IoU 56.3→58.1 and traffic-sign 46.8→45.6. On off-road Rellis-3D the same swap *cut* pole IoU from 56.1 to 52.3 while raising mIoU by 4.0 ([Viswanath et al., Tables I and III](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.13188)). That suggests the raw range-dependent brightness pattern itself is a pole cue. Classic MLS road-furniture work at ITC Twente shows a complementary split. A high-reflectance ratio identifies sign faces, but "signs scanned from the side lack high reflectance", and geometry carries bare poles and luminaires ([Li, Oude Elberink & Vosselman 2017](https://isprs-archives.copernicus.org/articles/XLII-2-W7/247/2017/isprs-archives-XLII-2-W7-247-2017.pdf)). That pattern is consistent with PoleSight's per-class split: intensity helps `fence_pole`, while range helps `traffic_pole` (15/15) and `gantry_sign_pole` (14/15). The mechanism is **inferred, Weak**: no study tested material- or type-discriminating pole subclasses. No range-view paper reports pole IoU with versus without intensity, and no range-view instance-segmentation paper reports any channel ablation. **Unknown.**

## Small data and one strong modality favour early and late fusion over multi-stage mid fusion

The general multimodal-detection literature gives a fuller picture of fusion *stage*, and it splits by data scale. On large RGB-thermal benchmarks, mid (feature-level) fusion usually beats naive channel concatenation. On KAIST with YOLOv5/ResNet50, pixel-level fusion scored 29.31 miss rate against 15.17 for feature-level fusion. That is worse than either RGB (18.39) or thermal (17.89) alone, with run-to-run standard deviations of 1.2–2.9 ([Zhou et al., bag of tricks, Table II](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.18288)). On M3FD with YOLOv11n, mid fusion led (55.38 AP) and early fusion was the worst of six strategies (52.66) ([YOLOv11-RGBT, Table 15](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.14696), secondary). That is the case for mid fusion, and on its home benchmarks (4k–50k image pairs, two physically different sensors, imperfect alignment) it is **established, Strong**.

Two findings break that pattern, and both match PoleSight's conditions more closely. First, **non-learned late fusion of separately tuned single-modality detectors beat learned mid fusion** on both KAIST and FLIR. On KAIST, Faster R-CNN scored LAMR 18.67 (RGB), 18.99 (thermal), 19.36 (early fusion), 14.48 (mid fusion), 10.78 (plain NMS over the two single-modality detectors) and **8.50 (ProbEn)**. On FLIR, every late-fusion variant (82.65–83.76 AP50) beat mid fusion (80.53) ([Chen et al., ECCV 2022, Tables 1, 3](https://www.ecva.net/papers/eccv_2022/papers_ECCV/papers/136690139.pdf)). The same paper found that **averaging scores fails** (19.4–19.5, because it "double counts" the class prior) and that temperature calibration of each detector was "crucial". Second, **on a small dataset with a small YOLO, early fusion beat every mid-fusion point.** On VEDAI (1,089 training images per fold), YOLOv5s scored 69.5 mAP50 with pixel concatenation and 70.3 with a pixel-level fusion module. Feature concatenation after blocks 1–4 scored 66.0/68.5/64.8/63.8, and **multi-stage feature fusion scored 59.3** at 70% more GFLOPs ([SuperYOLO, Table III](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.13351)). PoleSight has fewer training images (870) than one VEDAI fold. Status for "at ~1k images, early or single-shallow fusion ≥ multi-stage mid fusion": **established in one controlled study, Moderate**. Transfer to instance masks is inferred.

Instance segmentation has one controlled stage comparison, and it complicates the picture. On WISDOM (RGB-D), SF Mask R-CNN reached mask AP 59.0 (RGB), 59.6 (depth), **55.5 (early fusion, below both)**, 58.7 (late) and 60.5 (attention-weighted "confidence" fusion) ([SF Mask R-CNN README](https://github.com/gist-ailab/SF-Mask-RCNN); Moderate, README-level). So early fusion can lose, and the best learned fusion beat the best single modality by only 0.9 mask AP. The decisive difference from PoleSight is that RGB and depth come from different sensors with different statistics. PoleSight's two renderings share one projection, one set of returns and one grid. The only same-sensor range-view test of splitting the stem found that **two branches without extra modules matched stacking for SalsaNext (59.7 vs 59.7) and added +1.0 for RangeViT (59.6→60.6)**. The large benefits of branching appeared only out-of-domain, from weather-noise and reflectance-calibration modules ([Yang et al. 2025, Tables 4–5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.08979)). A two-stream network also gains from capacity alone. CMX scored 46.7 mIoU with RGB, 47.2 with RGB+RGB, **47.7 with RGB+noise**, 51.1 with RGB+depth and 52.0 with RGB+HHA ([CMX, Table XI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.04838)). Fusion does not even reliably beat the stronger modality when the second input is largely derived from the first. On KITTI-360, every bimodal CMX/CMNeXt/TokenFusion model scored *below* RGB-only SegFormer (67.04 mIoU). The authors attribute this to depth being derived from RGB, leaving "limited modal differences" ([DeLiVER, Table 1a](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.01480)).

Taken together, these results **contradict the earlier draft's choice of multi-scale two-stream mid fusion as the main candidate**. The evidence that would justify that choice (large data, distinct sensors, misalignment) is absent here. The evidence that matches PoleSight (small data, same sensor, one strong modality) favours stacking or late fusion. Mid fusion is still worth testing as a later tier, in its cheapest evidence-backed form. Fusing at a single node usually beat three-node mid fusion with fewer parameters: FLIR YOLOv11n 39.70 AP for P3-only versus 38.41 for multi-node ([YOLOv11-RGBT](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.14696), secondary). A ControlNet-style "MCF" design freezes a strong single-modality detector and injects the second modality through a zero-initialised convolution. It raised FLIR YOLOv11x from 41.96 (IR-only) to 47.61 AP. The design is built never to fall below its frozen base ([YOLOv11-RGBT](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.14696), secondary). For PoleSight even P3 is too coarse (next section), so any mid-fusion node belongs at stride 4 or earlier. That placement is inferred, Weak. Heavy fusion models carry 2–4× the parameters of a single-modality model, for example Fusion-Mamba-YOLOv8 at 287.6M against 76.7M ([arXiv 2404.09146](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.09146), secondary). They are validated only on 4k–50k-pair datasets, and their margins over *simple* concatenation (+1 to +4 points) are smaller than their margins over single modalities. At 870 images, they have **no supporting evidence**.

| Fusion stage | Best-matching evidence for PoleSight | Direction | Status, strength |

| Early (channel stack) | SuperYOLO VEDAI small-data ablation; Casado-Coscolla YOLOv8-seg on Ouster; range-view convention | ≥ mid fusion at ~1k images; ≥ best single channel on average | Established in analogues, Moderate |
| Late (decision) | ProbEn KAIST/FLIR; Ouster practitioners run per-channel YOLO | Beat mid fusion with tuned single-modality detectors; score averaging fails; calibration crucial | Established for boxes, Moderate; masks unknown |

## Resolution outweighs the fusion operator for 71 px² poles

PoleSight's objects are smaller than the scales most fusion research operates at. A median 71 px² mask that fills 0.35 of a box with height/width 2.1 implies a box about 9.8×20.6 px at native resolution. At `imgsz: 640` with `rect: false`, Ultralytics resizes the long side to 640, so the frame becomes 640×80 and the box about 6.1×12.9 px (simple arithmetic from the dataset statistics). That is under one cell of the stride-8 grid in width. Ultralytics YOLO-seg mask prototypes are fixed at imgsz/4, and `mask_ratio` cannot raise that limit ([ultralytics issue #26436](https://github.com/ultralytics/ultralytics/issues/26436)). So a 1–2 px pole shaft is narrower than one prototype pixel. Mask mAP50-95 of 8–12 against box mAP50-95 of 19–29 (`results/result.md`) fits a mask-resolution ceiling. That diagnosis is inferred, Moderate.

Every measured resolution lever is larger than every measured fusion lever. **In SuperYOLO itself, fusing IR into RGB added +2.6 mAP50** (72.49→75.09). Removing the stride-2 Focus stem added **+7.3 to +8.8**, and a training-only super-resolution branch added a further **+8.5** at unchanged inference cost ([SuperYOLO, Tables II, IV, VII](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.13351)). For range images specifically, halving horizontal resolution is costly. Moving from 64×2048 to 64×1024 cut CENet pole IoU from 61.5 to 55.9, and moving to 64×512 cut it to 49.5. Sign and trunk lost similar amounts ([RangeFormer, Table 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.05367v3)). PoleSight's 0.625× downscale sits between those rows. Training on native-resolution azimuth crops (RangeFormer's STR, 384-px views of 2048-wide scans) recovered or exceeded full-width accuracy: CENet scored 65.8 mIoU with STR versus 64.7 at full 2048. Slicing-aided fine-tuning and inference added +12.7 to +14.5 AP50 for three detectors on aerial benchmarks ([SAHI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06934)). Casado-Coscolla avoided downscaling their 1024×128 Ouster frames altogether by splitting each into four overlapping segments tiled into one 640×640 canvas for YOLOv8-seg ([PMC11728245](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11728245/)). That is a ready-made recipe for PoleSight's frame shape. Status for "effective resolution is a first-order lever for tiny thin objects": **established, Strong**. Its size for YOLO-seg masks on PoleSight is **unknown**, as is any measurement of P2 prototypes or SR branches on a YOLO-*seg* model.

Range-view architectures encode the same lesson structurally. RangeNet++ downsamples only horizontally ([RangeNet++](https://www.ipb.uni-bonn.de/wp-content/papercite-data/pdf/milioto2019iros.pdf)). CENet and RangeFormer use stride-1 stems and cap total stride at 8 ([CENet code](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/huixiancheng/CENet/main/modules/network/ResNet.py); [RangeFormer](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.05367v3)). RangeViT gained +4.3 mIoU from a convolutional stem in place of linear patch embedding. Rectangular 2×8 patches scored 75.21 mIoU against 68.45 for 16×16, and the authors note that small patches help "smaller and thinner objects" ([RangeViT, Tables 1, 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2301.10222v2)). A standard YOLO stem maps PoleSight's 80 downscaled rows to 10 rows at P3. No range-view segmentation network compresses the vertical axis that much. An anisotropic or stride-1 stem is **inferred, Moderate** to help, by convention and indirect ablations, but no isolated ablation exists. This also sets where fusion can act. Feature-level fusion at stride 8 or coarser merges features in which the pole is already sub-cell, and that is consistent with SuperYOLO's early-beats-late ordering for small vehicles. Any learned fusion should happen at the input or at stride ≤4 (inferred, Weak).

Two further tiny-object findings bear on model choice. **Label assignment by IoU collapses for very tiny boxes.** Faster R-CNN, Cascade R-CNN and DetectoRS all score AP_vt = 0.0 on AI-TOD. Replacing IoU with the Normalized Gaussian Wasserstein Distance lifts them by +4.9 to +6.7 AP, mostly through assignment ([NWD, Tables 2, 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2110.13389v1)). YOLOv8+ uses a CIoU-based task-aligned assigner, so similar sensitivity is plausible but **unmeasured** (inferred, Weak). **No detector family wins tiny objects everywhere.** Mask2Former beats Mask R-CNN on COCO overall but loses on AP_S (23.4 vs 23.8), and its authors concede it "struggles with segmenting small objects" ([Mask2Former](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2112.01527)). Dense-query DINO (900 queries, 25.9 AP) and DQ-DETR (30.2 AP) beat Cascade R-CNN (15.1 AP) on AI-TOD-V2 ([DQ-DETR, Table 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.03507v2)). Cascade R-CNN beats Deformable DETR on SODA-D (31.2 vs 19.2 AP) ([SODA, TPAMI 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2207.14096), secondary). No same-protocol comparison of YOLO-seg against Mask R-CNN or Mask2Former on a tiny-object *instance segmentation* benchmark was found. So the evidence does not justify replacing the Ultralytics family before its resolution limits are removed. A query-based alternative should use dense queries and stride-4 masks (inferred, Moderate).

## The training recipe decides whether fusion clears the range-only bar

### Encoding 16-bit renderings into 8 bits is a large, unsolved choice

The current baselines collapse 18,754 distinct 16-bit values to 167 levels through `cv2.IMREAD_COLOR` and replicate them across three identical channels (`results/result.md`). Thermal detection shows how much the squeeze matters. On FLIR, feeding raw 14-bit data scored 20.1/37.9/12.3 mAP (RetinaNet/YOLOX/Sparse-RCNN), against up to 25.2/53.5/39.1 for 8-bit tone maps. The fixed tone maps (AGC, 1–99% clip, histogram equalisation, min-max) spread by **up to 12.8 mAP on YOLOX**, with no single winner across detectors. A 3-channel stack of different tone maps of the *same* signal beat every single tone map for two of three detectors (29.1/53.3/41.6), and a learned colouriser was best (34.7/56.0/45.7) ([Thermal Chameleon, Table II](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18340)). For depth with ImageNet CNNs, HHA beat fine-tuned replicated disparity by 5.1 AP (25.2 vs 20.1) ([Gupta et al. 2014, Table 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1407.5736)). Jet, normals and HHA beat grey depth by 1.0–2.7 points on classification, and HHA lost its edge when its height channel carried no information ([Eitel et al. 2015, Table III](https://arxiv.org/abs/1507.06821)). LiDAR-as-camera papers use histogram equalisation for reflectivity and ambient and fixed-maximum linear clamping for range ([Casado-Coscolla](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11728245/); [Tampuu et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.15170)), or intensity / inverse depth / depth in R/G/B, chosen "after a qualitative analysis" ([Dai et al.](https://opus.lib.uts.edu.au/rest/bitstreams/9c4b4e78-897e-452c-ac3f-39ce4ec2aafc/retrieve)). None of them compared alternatives. Status: "encoding is a first-order factor" is **established on thermal and depth, Moderate**. *Which* encoding is best for LiDAR range and intensity images (linear, percentile, log, inverse, histogram-equalised, multi-encoding stack) is **unknown**. No study measured log or inverse range for range-view networks. The earlier draft recommended log or inverse range as the encoding, but that recommendation had no evidence behind it and should become a tested factor. The detector-dependence of the thermal ranking means encodings should be compared on more than one YOLO variant.

### Keep the pretrained stem, or replace it deliberately

When `channels != 3`, Ultralytics silently leaves the first convolution random. A 2- or 4-channel run therefore changes the input and the stem at once (verified code fact). The evidence on how much this costs is indirect. ImageNet pretraining helps range-view networks by +0.5 to +1.5 mIoU ([RangeFormer, Table 6](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.05367)) and by +2.4 to +2.8 for RangeViT, whose stem is *always* randomly initialised. A trained convolutional stem in front of a pretrained trunk is a large win there (+4.3) ([RangeViT, Tables 1, 4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.10222)). A one-channel depth image replicated into an RGB-pretrained network still transfers (11.3 AP with no fine-tuning, 20.1 with it) ([Gupta et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1407.5736)). Bolting a second modality onto an RGB-only-pretrained network can make fusion *worse* than the single modality: MAE scored 49.3 mIoU with RGB-D against 50.8 with RGB, while multimodal pretraining (MultiMAE) turned that into 56.0 ([MultiMAE, Table 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.01678)). A backbone pretrained on depth beat an RGB-pretrained one by 15.2 mIoU on depth-only input ([DFormer, Table 12](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.09668)). The practical consequences are inferred (Moderate). The 3-channel stack keeps the COCO stem and is the cheapest safe path. Any two-stream design should warm-start each branch from its own single-modality PoleSight checkpoint rather than from COCO, which is the analogue of modality-matched pretraining. No controlled detection or segmentation comparison of replicated, mean-of-RGB-filter or random initialisation for an extra input channel was found. The earlier drafts' "mean of the RGB filters" initialisation is a reasonable method that has never been measured (**unknown**).

### Do not balance a dominant modality; prioritise it, drop it occasionally, or distil from the fused model

The modality-imbalance literature (OGM-GE, AGM, PMR, MMPareto, MLA, ReconBoost) is evaluated almost entirely on classification. BalanceBenchmark's seven datasets are all classification tasks ([arXiv 2502.10816](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.10816)), and no detection or segmentation results for these methods were found. The one result that matches PoleSight's strong/weak regime is a warning. On AVE, where audio (62.16) is about twice as accurate as visual (31.40), OGM-GE (65.62), AGM (64.50) and PMR (63.62) all fell *below* plain concatenation (66.15). Methods that keep the dominant modality strong (MLA 70.92, PDMP 71.28) did best ([PDMP, Table I](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.05773)). BalanceBenchmark's own authors report that "greater balance between modalities does not guarantee better performance". This **contradicts the earlier draft's Tier-5 suggestion to adapt OGM-GE-style gradient modulation**. The better-supported options are unimodal auxiliary heads or losses on each branch, or an asymmetric weight favouring the range branch. That is inferred from classification, Weak, but it points the opposite way from gradient damping.

**Modality dropout** is roughly neutral on full-input accuracy and strongly protective when an input is missing. ModDrop's full-modality accuracy went from 96.77% to 96.81% while missing-input accuracy rose by up to 20 points ([ModDrop, Table 9](https://chriswolfvision.github.io/www/papers/pami2015.pdf)). Condition dropout added +0.1 to +1.0 mIoU on full RGB-D input ([ConD, Table I](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20326), secondary). UniBEV found that dropping *only* the weak modality was worse than a mix that sometimes drops the strong one ([UniBEV](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.14516), secondary). For PoleSight, its value is as a guard that keeps a fused model from falling below range-only. Occasionally dropping range forces the intensity pathway to learn the `fence_pole` cue on its own (inferred, Moderate).

**Cross-modal distillation from a fused teacher** is the best-evidenced way to get fusion-level accuracy out of a single modality. In 2DPASS, a LiDAR-only student rose from 65.58 to 69.32 mIoU on SemanticKITTI val. Feature alignment alone gave 66.34, standard Hinton KD 66.34, and the authors attribute the gain "mainly" to the fused teacher's predictions. On nuScenes test, the distilled LiDAR-only student (80.8) beat two explicit camera-LiDAR fusion networks (77.0 and 80.0) ([2DPASS, Tables 1, 3–5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.04397)). Per-class gains on poles (+1.3) and signs (+0.2) were small next to bicycles (+12.5). Status: **established for camera→LiDAR semantic segmentation, Strong**. Its use for intensity→range on one sensor is **unknown**, and the expected pole gain is modest.

### Augmentation is larger than any fusion effect measured so far

Range-view mixing augmentation produces some of the largest gains anywhere in this evidence base. RangeAug (row-band mixing, union, tail-class paste, azimuthal shift) lifted FIDNet from 50.8 to 61.3, CENet from 56.2 to 62.4 and RangeFormer from 56.0 to 66.0 mIoU over plain training. That is +2.5 to +7.4 over standard augmentation ([RangeFormer, Fig. 5b](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.05367), read from figure). PolarMix raised MinkNet from 55.9 to 65.0, with pole 62.1→64.6 and traffic-sign 43.7→49.9. In a small-data regime (one training sequence), rotate-paste of instances added +4.3 against +2.0 for simple paste ([PolarMix, Tables 1, 6](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.00223)). With 1% labels, LaserMix lifted range-view pole IoU from 52.5 to 59.0 and traffic-sign from 35.7 to 41.7 ([LaserMix, Table 9](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.00026)). Status: **established for range-view semantic segmentation, Strong**. Transfer to 2D YOLO-seg on fixed renderings is **unknown**. The applicable forms are horizontal roll of the 360° frame, swapping row bands or azimuth sectors between frames, and pasting pole instances at the *same rows* with range and intensity moved together. Standard YOLO augmentation conflicts with these semantics. With packed channels, `RandomHSV` remixes intensity and range differently for each sample (verified code fact), so `hsv_h` and `hsv_s` must be zero for fused runs. No study measured photometric jitter on LiDAR renderings.

## An evidence-ranked plan, and what only PoleSight can answer

The ladder below orders experiments by evidence strength and cost. Each rung is also a control for the next. **Rung 0 is measurement.** Bag-of-tricks reports run-to-run standard deviations of 1.2–2.9 points on KAIST and module rankings that flip with the backbone ([arXiv 2411.18288](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.18288)), and SuperYOLO used 10-fold cross-validation on a dataset of PoleSight's size. The expected fusion effect (one or two mask points) is the same size as that noise. Every comparison therefore needs at least three seeds, and the headline delta needs a paired bootstrap over test images. Per-class claims need particular care: the test split has only 95 `fence_pole` and 78 `gantry_sign_pole` instances (`data/dataset_analysis_report.json`).

**Rung 1 retrains the single-modality baselines at native effective resolution.** The options are `imgsz: 1024` with rectangular batches, a ×2 upscale, or Casado-Coscolla-style four-segment tiling into 640×640, optionally with a P2 prototype branch. These become the bars fusion must clear. The evidence that resolution matters is Strong; its size on YOLO-seg masks is unknown. **Rung 2 is early fusion with the stem intact:** a 3-channel image such as [intensity, range, validity mask] or [intensity, range, a second range encoding], with `hsv_h: 0` and `hsv_s: 0`. A matched [range, range, mask] control separates the intensity contribution from the mask contribution. Evidence is Moderate (Casado-Coscolla, SuperYOLO, the SalsaNet/SqueezeSegV2 mask results). **Rung 3 is late fusion** of the two rung-1 models using ProbEn or NMS scoring with temperature calibration. It should be paired with a two-seed range+range ensemble so that modality gain is separated from ensembling gain. Evidence is Moderate for boxes. Mask merging (for example, IoU-matched mask-probability averaging) has no published recipe and would be a small contribution in itself. **Rung 4 tunes the recipe on the winning input:** range-view augmentation (roll, row-band mix, same-row instance paste), intensity and range encodings tested as factors, hole filling with the mask kept, and modality dropout as a guard. **Rung 5 runs only if a gap remains:** a single fusion node at stride ≤4 or an MCF-style frozen-range-branch with a zero-initialised intensity injection, each branch warm-started from its own PoleSight checkpoint. It must be tested against a capacity-matched single stream and a duplicated-input two-stream control, because CMX shows noise alone buys +1.0. For deployment, a 2DPASS-style distillation of the best fused model into a range-only student is the best-evidenced option. **Rung 6 is research novelty, not expected accuracy:** geometry-prior attention in the style of DFormerv2, GeminiFusion pixel-wise attention, or a dense-query DETR with stride-4 masks.

Several choices have no evidence for this setting and should not be run first. Gradient-balancing methods (OGM-GE, AGM, PMR) have measured harm when one modality dominates. Multi-stage two-stream fusion has measured harm at small data scale. ViT backbones with square /14–/16 patches lose 6.8 mIoU on nuScenes range images (16×16 versus 2×8 patches in RangeViT), and PoleSight's targets are far smaller. Calibration or alignment modules have nothing to correct on a shared pixel grid.

| Question PoleSight would answer first | Why no existing source answers it |
|---|---|
| Does intensity + range beat range-only for **instance segmentation of thin poles**, and by how much per class? | No range-view instance-segmentation channel ablation exists; Dong et al.'s range-image pole segmenter is range-only and binary ([arXiv 2208.07364](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.07364)) |
| Does early, late or shallow-mid fusion win on **two renderings of one MLS scanner** at ~1k images? | All fusion-stage ablations use two physical sensors; the only same-sensor test is semantic segmentation (Yang 2025) |

| How much of PoleSight's low mask AP is prototype-resolution-limited, and do P2 prototypes, SR branches or NWD assignment fix it? | No YOLO-seg tiny-object study reports these |

## Conclusion

The literature changes what counts as success for PoleSight fusion. The expected gain is about one to two mask points. Same-sensor stacking in comparable work bought +0.4 to +2.6, and PoleSight's own range-over-intensity margin is +1.28. That is the same size as seed noise and smaller than the levers sitting next to it: resolution, augmentation and encoding each moved published results by roughly 5–15 points. So the risk in the earlier plan was less a wrong architecture choice than measuring fusion on top of a resolution-starved, noise-dominated baseline, where any architecture would look like noise. The most defensible paper claim is a carefully controlled one: native-resolution baselines, three or more seeds, early and late fusion with duplicate-modality and capacity controls, and per-class reporting that can show whether intensity's `fence_pole` advantage survives fusion.

The evidence also changes which novelty is worth chasing. The well-funded RGB-X fusion modules are built for large data, distinct sensors and misalignment, and PoleSight has none of these. PoleSight's open questions are different: what a same-sensor modality pair contributes to thin-object *instance* masks, how 16-bit LiDAR renderings should be encoded for pretrained detectors, and whether range-view augmentation and fused-teacher distillation carry over to 2D instance segmentation. These are questions where a modest dataset can still produce the first published evidence, because nobody has measured them.
