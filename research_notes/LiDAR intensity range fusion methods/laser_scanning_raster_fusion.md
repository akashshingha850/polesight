# Fusion of laser-scanning-derived raster modalities (intensity + range/height + geometric channels) for detection and segmentation, with focus on pole-like and roadside objects

Scope note: notes compiled 2026-10-04. Evidence levels are marked: **[FT]** = read in the full text (table/section cited), **[ABS]** = abstract, search snippet or secondary citation only (not verified against the paper body). Several primary sources (ScienceDirect, MDPI, ResearchGate) refused automated access (HTTP 403), which limited how many numbers could be checked. Those are listed under Gaps.

Context for the reader (not researched): PoleSight has 1,088 pixel-registered 1024x128 range images from Finnish MLS, with an intensity rendering and a range rendering, 4 pole classes, instance polygons, and tiny thin objects.

---

## Q1. Which studies quantify intensity vs elevation/height/range vs combined LiDAR rasters for detection/segmentation, and with what numbers?

### Takeaway
I found only a few controlled LiDAR-only channel ablations with numbers. The ones I could verify come from the range-view segmentation literature (SalsaNext on Rellis-3D/SemanticKITTI; Waymo range images), not from photogrammetric MLS pole work. They show that intensity/reflectivity changes mIoU by about 0–4 points overall, and the pole class does not always benefit. Remote-sensing papers on ALS/MLS rasters (road markings, manholes, buildings, trees) mostly pick channels by design (intensity rasters for road-surface objects; height/density for buildings and trees). Where they compare channel combinations at all, the comparisons are thin, and the numbers were not accessible to me.

### Cited Findings

**Range-view LiDAR-only channel ablations (closest analogue to PoleSight's intensity+range renderings)**
- [FT] Viswanath, Jiang, Saripalli, "Reflectivity Is All You Need!: Advancing LiDAR Semantic Segmentation" (arXiv 2403.13188; IFAC-PapersOnLine 2025). They used SalsaNext on range images with the input configurations rxyzi (range, x, y, z, raw intensity), rxyzn (near-range-calibrated reflectivity in place of intensity), rxyzirn (all three) and rxyzi+Gaussian-noise. **Table I, Rellis-3D (off-road): mIoU 38.4 (rxyzi) / 42.4 (rxyzn) / 41.2 (rxyzirn) / 41.9 (rxyzi gau)**. The **Pole class IoU went down** when raw intensity was replaced: 56.1 (rxyzi) / 52.3 (rxyzn) / 51.0 (rxyzirn) / 52.5 (gau). **Table III, SemanticKITTI val: mIoU 54.0 / 54.2 / 54.8 / 53.7**, i.e. differences under 1 point in urban data. **Table II, cross-sensor (train Ouster OS-64, test Velodyne VLP-32)**: pole IoU 0.1 (rxyzi) vs 1.1 (rxyzn). Pole transfer collapses either way. — [arXiv 2403.13188](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.13188); [ScienceDirect version](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2405896325003477)
- [FT] Guillard et al., "Learning to Simulate Realistic LiDARs" (arXiv 2209.10986), Sec. IV-D "Is intensity useful?". In Tab. III, a segmentation CNN trained on real Waymo range images **with** the intensity channel performs better than one trained without it. Tab. IV shows the same for networks trained on their synthetic range images. (I could not reliably parse the IoU values out of the PDF layout; only the direction is verified.) — [arXiv 2209.10986](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.10986)
- [FT] Dong, Chen, Särkkä, Stachniss, "Online pole segmentation on range images for long-term LiDAR localization in urban environments", Robotics and Autonomous Systems 2023 (arXiv 2208.07364). The geometric extractor uses **range only** (Sec. 2 says the earlier conference version "uses only range information"). The learned extractor is SalsaNext retrained as binary pole/non-pole on "range images plus the pseudo pole labels", with input fixed at 32x256 and "valid range values normalized between 0 and 1" (Sec. 3.3, Sec. 4 setup). **No intensity-vs-range ablation is reported.** — [arXiv 2208.07364](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.07364); [code](https://github.com/PRBonn/pole-localization)

**MLS-derived rasters for road-surface objects (intensity-dominant)**
- [ABS] Wen et al., "A deep learning framework for road marking extraction, classification and completion from mobile laser scanning point clouds", ISPRS J. Photogramm. Remote Sens. (2019). A modified U-Net segments road-marking pixels in MLS intensity images to cope with intensity variation and low contrast. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0924271618302855)
- [ABS] "A two-stage approach for road marking extraction and modeling using MLS point clouds" (2021). U-Net on a raster whose feature maps are per-pixel **average intensity, elevation variance, and distance from points to scanner centre**. The snippet says "intensity is the most distinctive feature". I did not see whether a channel ablation was run. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/354198933_A_two-stage_approach_for_road_marking_extraction_and_modeling_using_MLS_point_clouds)
- [ABS] Recent road-marking work keeps the MLS intensity image as the sole input for oriented detection with channel/spatial attention and for instance segmentation with context and boundary refinement. — [Oriented Road Marking Detection in MLS Intensity Images](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/391467583_Oriented_Road_Marking_Detection_in_MLS_Point_Cloud_Intensity_Images_Using_Channel_and_Spatial_Attention); [Int. J. Digital Earth 2025](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/17538947.2025.2531842)
- [ABS] Yu et al., manhole cover detection from MLS. Road points are rasterised into georeferenced intensity images by IDW, then classified with a convolutional capsule network on superpixel patches (or deep features + Random Forest), and delineated with a marked point process. — [Waterloo PDF](https://uwaterloo.ca/geospatial-intelligence/sites/default/files/uploads/files/road_manhole_cover_delineation_using_mobile.pdf); [Automated Detection of Urban Road Manhole Covers](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/275235267_Automated_Detection_of_Urban_Road_Manhole_Covers_Using_Mobile_Laser_Scanning_Data)
- [FT] Toronto-3D (Tan et al., CVPRW 2020). The baselines use coordinates only, and road-marking IoU is 0.00–18.78 across 7 methods (Table 3). Sec. 5.2 says "road markings on pavements are difficult to distinguish with point coordinates only, and additional color and intensity information would make road markings easier to identify". This is qualitative: no with/without-intensity run is reported. Pole IoU with coordinates only ranges from 56.86 (DGCNN) to 73.06 (KPFCNN) (Table 3). — [arXiv 2003.08284](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.08284)

**ALS rasters (buildings/trees)**
- [ABS] Maltezos et al., "Building Extraction From LiDAR Data Applying Deep Convolutional Neural Networks", IEEE GRSL 16:155–159 (2019). A CNN on an image stack of LiDAR-derived features (snippets list entropy, height variation, intensity, normalized height and related features) is used to find good input representations. Per-feature numbers were not accessible. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/327658541_Building_Extraction_From_LiDAR_Data_Applying_Deep_Convolutional_Neural_Networks); [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Building-Extraction-From-LiDAR-Data-Applying-Deep-Maltezos-Doulamis/6d4f1700d62a17ae3b9ea6045d293eeb23208372)
- [ABS/secondary] Windrim & Bryson, Remote Sensing 12(9):1469 (2020). Faster R-CNN tree detection from ALS BEV rasters with channels **vertical density (vd), max height (mh), mean return/intensity (mr)**. The reported result is that **vd alone performed best**, and the vd/mh/mr stack was "similar but slightly worse" at both sites. Adding height and intensity did not help over a density channel. — [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/12/9/1469) (summary via search; full text blocked); the earlier IROS version (arXiv 1810.12536) tested only the vertical-density raster: F1 0.933–1.000 on 4 plots, Table I [FT] — [arXiv 1810.12536](https://arxiv.org/html/1810.12536v1)
- [ABS] Building detection with LiDAR plus orthophoto: nDSM in the fusion beat other feature combinations and reduced FP/FN. This includes camera-derived imagery, so it is only partially in scope. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/326287322_Deep_Learning_Approach_for_Building_Detection_Using_LiDAR-Orthophoto_Fusion)

### Inferences
- The verified range-view numbers suggest intensity gives a small, class-dependent gain. Raw vs calibrated intensity moves mIoU by about 0–4 points, and the pole class went the other way on Rellis-3D (raw intensity was best for poles). For PoleSight, an "intensity only vs range only vs stacked" ablation is worth running. The literature does not predict a large, uniform gain from adding intensity for poles.
- In MLS remote sensing, the modality is chosen by object type. Flat, painted or retro-reflective targets (road markings, manholes, sign faces) are handled almost entirely with intensity rasters, and vertical/volumetric objects (trees, buildings, poles) with height/density/range. Pole-like objects with attached signs or luminaires span both regimes, which is an argument for keeping both channels.
- The Windrim & Bryson result (single density channel beats a 3-channel stack) is a reminder that naive early stacking of correlated LiDAR channels can fail to help or slightly hurt on small datasets. That matters at PoleSight's scale (1,088 images).

### Gaps
- I found **no published study that ablates intensity vs range vs both specifically for pole segmentation/detection on MLS range images**. Dong et al. 2023 reports no channel ablation.
- I did not find an ALS paper with a clean "intensity-only vs nDSM-only vs intensity+nDSM" deep-learning table I could verify. Candidate papers (Maltezos 2019; Windrim & Bryson 2020) were paywalled or blocked, so their tables are unverified.
- The numerical values in Guillard et al. Tab. III/IV (with vs without intensity) could not be parsed reliably.

## Q2. Deep learning on MLS pole-like objects (FGI/Aalto, WHU, TU Delft/ITC, Bonn/Stachniss, Paris-Lille-3D, Toronto-3D): input channels and helpful fusion

### Takeaway
Deep-learning pole work in LiDAR splits into four groups. (a) Range-image segmentation with range-only input (Dong/Chen/Stachniss). (b) 3D detectors on learned point/pillar encodings (Plachetka). (c) Few-shot point classifiers (Pole-NN). (d) Classic hand-crafted pipelines (ITC Twente: Li/Oude Elberink/Vosselman) that explicitly combine geometry (eigenvalues, size) with a reflectance ratio. None of the verified papers reports a controlled fusion ablation of intensity vs geometry for poles. The ITC work gives qualitative evidence that reflectance helps for sign faces but is unreliable when signs are scanned edge-on.

### Cited Findings
- [FT] **Dong, Chen, Särkkä, Stachniss (RAS 2023; Aalto + Bonn)**. Geometric pole extractor working directly on range images: clusters pixels by range similarity to neighbours, then filters clusters by size, aspect ratio (height larger than width) and height extent max(z)−min(z) (Sec. 3.2). Range image sizes are 32x256 (NCLT) and 64x500 (KITTI/MulRan). Pseudo labels from that extractor were used to train SalsaNext for binary pole segmentation (Sec. 3.3). **Table 1 (pole extraction P/R/F1):** NCLT: Schaefer et al. 0.690/0.386/0.495; geometric (Ours-G) 0.765/0.657/**0.706**; learned (Ours-L) 0.675/**0.674**/0.674. SemanticKITTI: Schaefer 0.621/0.380/0.455; Ours-G 0.687/0.439/0.515; Ours-L 0.607/0.582/**0.594**. The learned model raises recall at some cost in precision. The authors say the network is trained once on pseudo labels from several datasets and generalises without fine-tuning (Sec. 4). — [arXiv 2208.07364](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.07364); [Aalto portal](https://research.aalto.fi/en/publications/online-pole-segmentation-on-range-images-for-long-term-lidar-loca/)
- [FT, via related-work statements in Dong 2023 and Pole-NN] **Plachetka et al., "DNN-Based Recognition of Pole-Like Objects in LiDAR Point Clouds", IEEE ITSC 2021**: a DNN with learned point-cloud encodings (elements of PointPillars, VoxelNet and an SSD head) on their own dataset, reporting **classification accuracy 0.93 excluding billboard poles**. Input channels are not verified. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/355578032_DNN-Based_Recognition_of_Pole-Like_Objects_in_LiDAR_Point_Clouds); cited in [Pole-NN, ISPRS Annals X-4/W5-2024](https://isprs-annals.copernicus.org/articles/X-4-W5-2024/333/2024/isprs-annals-X-4-W5-2024-333-2024.pdf)
- [FT] **Zhang, Khoshelham, Shojaei, "Pole-NN: Few-Shot Classification of Pole-Like Objects in Lidar Point Clouds", ISPRS Annals X-4/W5-2024 (Melbourne)**: a non-parametric (Point-NN-style) few-shot classifier needing as little as one support sample per class. It introduces the **Parkville-3D** fine-grained PLO dataset and is also evaluated on Paris-Lille-3D, which, the authors note, lacks pole subclasses. Related work notes that Li et al. (2019a) used **size, eigenvalue-based and radiometric** features for PLO classification. — [ISPRS Annals PDF](https://isprs-annals.copernicus.org/articles/X-4-W5-2024/333/2024/isprs-annals-X-4-W5-2024-333-2024.pdf)
- [FT] **Li, Oude Elberink, Vosselman (ITC Twente), "Semantic labelling of road furniture in MLS data", ISPRS Archives XLII-2/W7, 2017**: rule/knowledge-based labelling of decomposed pole components using geometric structure (eigenvalue linear/planar/scattered), size/shape ratios, relative height/position, and a **"ratio of high reflectance"** feature (reflectance threshold tuned to 65 on dataset A) (Sec. 3). Results (Sec. 4.3, Tables 2–3): 94.3% of street-light heads detected (50 of 65 detections correct); traffic signs 50.9% recognised (27/36 correct); street signs 41.2%; traffic information signs 27.3%; traffic lights 51.6%. The authors attribute misses to unreliable geometric and reflectance attributes on small or incompletely scanned signs, and note that **signs scanned from the side lack high reflectance**. Traffic lights get confused with signs because of high planarity. Not deep learning, but it is the clearest MLS evidence on how the intensity and geometric cues complement each other and where each fails. — [ISPRS Archives PDF](https://isprs-archives.copernicus.org/articles/XLII-2-W7/247/2017/isprs-archives-XLII-2-W7-247-2017.pdf)
- [ABS] Li, Lehtomäki, Oude Elberink, Vosselman et al., "Semantic segmentation of road furniture in mobile laser scanning data", ISPRS J. Photogramm. Remote Sens. (2019): addresses multi-class road furniture such as a sign mounted on a street-light pole. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S092427161930142X). [ABS] Related: "Pole-like road furniture detection in sparse and unevenly distributed MLS data", ISPRS Annals IV-2 (2018), motivated by eigenvalue features being unreliable in sparse MLS. — [ISPRS Annals](https://isprs-annals.copernicus.org/articles/IV-2/185/2018/isprs-annals-IV-2-185-2018.pdf)
- [ABS] FGI/Aalto: the FGI Roamer system has been developed since 2003 for automatic road-environment extraction, and the classic vehicle-based pole extraction (Lehtomäki et al., Remote Sensing 2010) is non-DL. A review notes earlier lamp-post/light-pole detection rates of 62–86%. — [Lehtomäki 2010](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs2030641); [Object Classification and Recognition From MLS (FGI)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/282588807_Object_Classification_and_Recognition_From_Mobile_Laser_Scanning_Point_Clouds_in_a_Road_Environment)
- [ABS] **Reichler, Taher, Hyyppä, Kukko et al. (FGI), "Semantic segmentation of raw multispectral laser scanning data from urban environments with deep neural networks", ISPRS Open J. Photogramm. Remote Sens. 12 (2024)**: real-time (250 Hz) segmentation of raw, non-georeferenced 2D scanner data from the AkhkaR4-DW backpack (905 nm + 1550 nm). It reports 62.1 mIoU over 13 urban classes and uses single-wavelength and multispectral inputs. This is the most PoleSight-like Finnish deep-learning work (raw scanner-geometry input, intensity channels), but I could not access its tables to confirm the channel ablations. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2667393224000048); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/378673430_Semantic_segmentation_of_raw_multispectral_laser_scanning_data_from_urban_environments_with_deep_neural_networks)
- [FT] Sun, Li, Ye, Tanaka (Univ. Fukui), arXiv 2305.06845 (2023): deep-learning pole classification for pole-map matching/localisation on NCLT. No channel ablation found. — [arXiv 2305.06845](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.06845)
- [ABS] Traffic-sign work on MLS usually detects signs in 3D through retro-reflective intensity and then classifies them in co-registered camera images with a DNN. — [Exploiting synergies of mobile mapping sensors and deep learning for TSR (Expert Syst. Appl.)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0957417417305195); [Traffic sign detection in MLS point clouds (ISPRS J. 2016)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0924271616000368)

### Inferences
- The verified pole deep-learning work in range-image form (Dong et al.) is range-only and binary. PoleSight's 4-class instance task with intensity is not covered by an existing benchmark-style result, so even a basic intensity/range/stacked ablation would be new evidence.
- The ITC findings point to a **class-dependent** role for intensity. It helps with sign heads (retro-reflective) but is unreliable for edge-on signs, and it contributes little for bare poles and luminaires, which geometry identifies. In a fusion design, that is a reason to let the network weight the modalities per class or region (attention or gating) rather than relying on fixed stacking alone. This is a hypothesis, not a tested result.

### Gaps
- No verified table from FGI/Aalto/Oulu deep-learning pole work with channel ablations. The Reichler et al. 2024 tables were inaccessible.
- WHU pole-specific deep-learning papers with modality ablations were not found in the time available (see Q4 for WHU-Infra3D).
- I found no TU Delft deep-learning pole paper with modality ablations. The relevant Dutch group (ITC Twente) is non-DL.
- Plachetka 2021: input encodings (whether intensity was used) not verified.

## Q3. Do geometric derived channels (normals, curvature, verticality, height above ground, local range gradient) add to intensity+range?

### Takeaway
The direct evidence is weak. Geometric features are central in classic pole pipelines (eigenvalue linearity/planarity, size, height extent, range-discontinuity clustering), and the point-cloud literature reports that removing hand-crafted features (linearity, planarity, verticality) hurts deep models, but in indoor or ALS settings. I found no verified study showing that normals/curvature/range-gradient channels on top of intensity+range improve pole segmentation in range images.

### Cited Findings
- [FT] Dong et al. 2023 build the pole extractor from range-image geometry alone: neighbour range differences for clustering, then cluster size, height-to-width and max(z)−min(z) height filters (Sec. 3.2). On NCLT it beats the learned SalsaNext model in F1 (0.706 vs 0.674, Table 1). Hand-designed geometric cues on range images are competitive for poles. — [arXiv 2208.07364](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.07364)
- [FT] Li et al. 2017 (ITC) rely on eigenvalue-based structure (linear/planar/scattered), size ratios, relative height and reflectance ratio. Planarity confuses traffic lights with signs (Sec. 4.3). — [ISPRS Archives](https://isprs-archives.copernicus.org/articles/XLII-2-W7/247/2017/isprs-archives-XLII-2-W7-247-2017.pdf)
- [ABS] The 2018 ITC paper's motivation is that eigenvalue-based features are not robust in sparse and unevenly distributed MLS data. — [ISPRS Annals IV-2 2018](https://isprs-annals.copernicus.org/articles/IV-2/185/2018/isprs-annals-IV-2-185-2018.pdf)
- [ABS] Mérizette et al., "3D Feature Engineering for Deep Semantic Segmentation of Indoor Point Clouds", Photogrammetric Record (2026): adding hand-crafted features (linearity, planarity, scattering, verticality) to raw coordinates improves deep segmentation, and removing them hurts. This is **indoor** data. — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/phor.70047)
- [FT] In Viswanath et al., where a channel already encodes the information (x, y, z and range are all present), adding a third intensity variant (rxyzirn) did not consistently beat two (SemanticKITTI 54.8 vs 54.2; Rellis 41.2 vs 42.4; Tables I, III). Redundant channels give diminishing or negative returns. — [arXiv 2403.13188](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.13188)
- [ABS/secondary] Windrim & Bryson 2020: a density channel alone beat density+max height+mean intensity for ALS tree detection. — [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/12/9/1469)

### Inferences
- For PoleSight's two renderings (intensity, range), the cheapest geometric channels to derive are horizontal/vertical range gradients (depth discontinuities that outline thin poles) and, if per-pixel elevation can be recovered, a height-like channel. The evidence that these help a CNN is indirect. The range-gradient idea mirrors the clustering criterion that makes Dong et al.'s geometric extractor work, but no deep-learning ablation of it was found.
- Given the redundancy findings, derived channels should be tested one at a time against the 2-channel baseline rather than all stacked at once.

### Gaps
- No verified MLS/range-image study isolating normals, curvature, verticality or range gradient as extra CNN inputs for poles or street furniture.
- No verified study on "height above ground" as a range-image channel for poles.

## Q4. Datasets/benchmarks for roadside infrastructure from LiDAR that report modality ablations

### Takeaway
The main roadside LiDAR datasets (Paris-Lille-3D, Toronto-3D, SemanticKITTI, the new WHU-Infra3D) provide intensity (and sometimes RGB) but do **not** report systematic intensity/geometry ablations in their dataset papers. Toronto-3D only argues qualitatively that intensity/colour would help road markings.

### Cited Findings
- [FT] Toronto-3D (Tan et al., CVPRW 2020): Optech Maverick 32-line MLS, ~1 km, 78.3 M points, 8 classes including Road marking, Utility line and Pole ("utility poles, traffic signs, lamp posts"). Points carry intensity, RGB and GPS time (Table 1, Sec. 3). Baselines (Table 3, coordinates only): mIoU 49.60 (DGCNN) to 60.96 (MS-TGNet); Pole IoU 56.86–73.06; Road marking IoU 0.00–18.78. No intensity ablation, only the qualitative statement in Sec. 5.2. Table 1 also lists Paris-Lille-3D (2018, x, y, z, intensity, 1940 m, 143.1 M points, 50 classes) and SemanticKITTI (x, y, z, intensity, 39.2 km, 4.5 B points, 28 classes). — [arXiv 2003.08284](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.08284)
- [FT via HTML summary] **WHU-Infra3D** (Liu, Fu, Feng, Dong, Yang; LIESMARS Wuhan University; arXiv 2606.09882, June 2026): a multi-modal roadside-infrastructure benchmark captured with three MMS platforms (AS-900HL Shanghai 2018, HiScan-Z Wuhan 2021, KZH1750 Nanjing 2024) plus 8192x4096 panoramic cameras. It has 10 classes (traffic sign, street light, signal light, surveillance camera, cylindrical bollard, fire hydrant, trash bin, manhole, traffic cone, spherical bollard) and 5 tasks: 2D detection, cross-view matching, 3D geo-identification, point-cloud segmentation and attribute recognition (Table 4, Sec. 4.1). It evaluates Faster R-CNN, DINO, YOLO, GLIP, YOLO-World, Grounding DINO and LVLMs. **No ablation isolating point features (intensity/xyz/normals/height) was found.** The authors note attribute recognition is "still largely image-dependent" (Sec. 5.3). — [arXiv 2606.09882](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.09882)
- [FT] Parkville-3D (Melbourne, 2024): a fine-grained pole-like-object dataset introduced with Pole-NN. No modality ablation. — [Pole-NN](https://isprs-annals.copernicus.org/articles/X-4-W5-2024/333/2024/isprs-annals-X-4-W5-2024-333-2024.pdf); [GitHub](https://github.com/Cipher-zzz/Parkville-3D)
- [FT] Dong et al. released a pole dataset (pseudo/annotated poles for NCLT/KITTI/MulRan) with their code. — [GitHub PRBonn/pole-localization](https://github.com/PRBonn/pole-localization)
- [FT] Rellis-3D and SemanticKITTI are the datasets with the most explicit verified intensity/reflectivity ablations, both from Viswanath et al. (see Q1). They are autonomous-driving or off-road data, not mapping-grade MLS. — [arXiv 2403.13188](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.13188)

### Inferences
- A PoleSight result with a clean modality ablation (intensity vs range vs fused, plus derived geometric channels) on mapping-grade MLS range images would fill a gap that the roadside-infrastructure benchmarks leave open.
- The dataset papers' choice of mIoU per class means "pole" is usually one merged class (Toronto-3D) or lacks subclasses (Paris-Lille-3D, per Pole-NN). Few benchmarks offer PoleSight's 4-class pole split.

### Gaps
- I did not verify whether KITTI-360 or IQmulus/TerraMobilita papers report modality ablations. The time and tool budget ran out before checking them.
- Later papers that benchmark on Toronto-3D/Paris-Lille-3D with "xyz vs xyz+intensity vs xyz+RGB" ablations likely exist (several point-cloud networks report such tables), but none were verified here.
- Fusion-strategy comparisons (early stacking vs dual-stream vs attention vs late fusion) for **LiDAR-only** channels in remote sensing were not found with verifiable numbers. One candidate, DS-RangeNet ("Lightweight Dual-Stream LiDAR Semantic Segmentation for Industrial Indoor Environments", Electronics 15(17):3983, 2026), appeared in search but was blocked (HTTP 403), so its streams and ablations are unverified. — [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2079-9292/15/17/3983)
